# Decision Points

## DP1: Rejection (what a client sees and can do after a decline)

**Choice:** The client sees a clear "Declined" status, the reason the creator picked (for example "Fully booked right now"), and two next steps: "Find similar gigs" (same category) and "Request again".

**Why:** A decline is where a marketplace loses a client. If the client only sees a red badge, they leave. Knowing the reason tells them whether to retry later or look elsewhere, and one click to similar gigs keeps them on the platform. Young creators also decline often because they have limited time, so the flow has to make declining feel normal, not like a failure.

## DP2: Double booking (can a gig take a new booking while one is Pending?)

**Choice:** Yes. Multiple Pending requests are allowed. Clients see how many others are waiting and their place in the queue. If a creator has already accepted one request, the dashboard warns before they accept another. The same client cannot send two Pending requests for the same gig.

**Why:** Creators are often students who check their inbox once a day, and blocking new bookings during that window would lose them leads. A creator can also compare offers and pick the best one. Showing the queue keeps clients informed, so nobody waits blindly.

## DP3: Discovery (how gigs are ranked)

**Choice:** The default sort is "Recommended". Score = recency + a fairness boost for gigs with 0 or 1 bookings. Users can switch to Newest, Price low to high, or Price high to low. New gigs show a "New creator" badge.

**Why:** A marketplace for young creators fails if early listers collect every booking while newcomers stay invisible. The boost gives new creators a fair first chance, while recency keeps the page fresh. The boost fades after a couple of bookings, so it does not permanently favor anyone. Users can still sort by price or date.
